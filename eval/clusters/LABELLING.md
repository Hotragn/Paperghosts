# Labelling reprint pairs (decision 36)

Each pair is two printings from American newspapers, 1770 to 1963, as AmericanStories' OCR gives them, often garbled. For each pair, decide whether the two are **one story**. You see each printing's paper, date, headline, and its first 400 words.

## same: one story

One printing is the other's text, printed again. Allow for all of these:
- OCR errors: misread letters and words, broken lines, stray characters
- cuts: one is shorter because paragraphs were dropped, or it stops early
- a new, missing or different headline
- a credit line, such as "From the Hartford Courant"
- paragraphs added or dropped at either end, such as a local introduction

An advertisement or notice printed again word for word is one story too.

## different: not one story

The two only share something smaller than a story:
- boilerplate: stock phrases, an advertisement's standard wording, a legal form, a masthead
- a quoted passage: the same verse, poem or quotation inside otherwise different articles
- different instalments of a serial: the same title, but different chapter text
- the same standing column or department with different content, such as "Local Brevities", or market reports of different days whose figures differ
- the same template with different particulars, such as testimonial advertisements quoting different people
- the same event told in different words, by different writers

Unrelated texts are different too.

## close: you can't decide

Use this when the text shown can't settle it. For example:
- it's too garbled or too short
- it's a borderline case, such as a table whose figures mostly agree

## What to write

For every pair, write its id, the label, and a note of fewer than 20 words. The note says what the two share or how they differ. A clear `same` may leave the note empty.

Judge only from the text shown. Don't guess from the papers or the dates.
